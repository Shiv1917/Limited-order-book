# ob-sim: known biases and limitations

Every simulator is optimistic somewhere and pessimistic somewhere else. This
file lists where `ob-sim` is each, and in which direction. Where a bias can be
switched off, the effect was measured on the AAPL 2019-01-30 data (09:30–09:58)
with the market-maker config `sim/configs/aapl_mm.json`.

## Biases

### No market impact
* **Direction:** optimistic, more so for larger size.
* **Why:** history never reacts to our orders. A quote we add does not
  discourage or attract anyone, and a take does not move anyone's quotes.
* **Mitigation:** keep size small relative to displayed depth. The
  market-maker quote is 100 shares, against a median top-level size of 200
  shares (mean 370) after 09:30:05.

### A strategy fill and the execution it rides on use the same volume
* **Direction:** optimistic fill rate.
* **Why:** a historical execution of q shares can fill our order *and* still
  execute X in the replay.
* **Mitigation:** the `fill_model.exec_volume_share` flag. At 0.5, the fill
  rate goes from 0.052 to 0.036 and net PnL from −$699 to −$315.

### Trade-through fills
* **Direction:** mixed, with an optimistic fill rate.
* **Why:** an execution at p fills our orders priced better than p, capped at
  the trade size rather than the aggressor's full size. This is the main
  reason the fill rate *rises* with latency: stale quotes end up through the
  market and get picked off.
* **Mitigation:** the `fill_model.trade_through` flag. Switching it off
  halves fill rate (0.052 → 0.024) and flips the latency trend to the
  expected fall. See [validation](sim_validation.md#6-behavioural-sanity).

### A historical limit order that crosses our resting order does not fill us
* **Direction:** pessimistic.
* **Why:** history added it without our order present, so it simply rests in
  the replay.
* **Mitigation:** only arises for quotes inside the spread or stale quotes.
  A later execution on our side can still fill us through the trade-through
  rule.

### Hidden executions never fill our orders
* **Direction:** pessimistic.
* **Why:** documented choice. Hidden volume is counted instead (571,251
  shares of type-5 volume on the day).
* **Mitigation:** none.

### Executions we cannot resolve to an order give no fill
* **Direction:** pessimistic.
* **Why:** documented choice; `unknown_exec_refs` counts them.
* **Mitigation:** none needed on this data, where the count is 0.

### Aggressive fills use displayed size
* **Direction:** slightly optimistic.
* **Why:** `own_taken` stops double counting our own takes, but cannot know
  whether someone else would have taken the same shares first.
* **Mitigation:** small orders. Turning `own_taken` off changes nothing for
  the market maker (identical output), because its takes are rare and small.

### Modify is cancel plus new and always loses priority
* **Direction:** pessimistic for strategies that resize.
* **Why:** there is no in-place size reduction for strategy orders.
* **Mitigation:** documented.

### Latency is a model, not measured
* **Direction:** either.
* **Why:** the delays are configured numbers.
* **Mitigation:** report a sweep (0 to 5 ms), not one value.

### Queue position is exact only within the data's own ordering
* **Direction:** either.
* **Why:** NASDAQ order types re-ranked by the exchange are not visible in
  the feed. 1.7% of AAPL executions were not at the front in our book; see
  `docs/design.md`.
* **Mitigation:** none possible from the feed.

### One ticker-day, and only 09:30–09:58 of it
* **Direction:** noise.
* **Why:** the free ITCH sample prefix ends at 09:58:57.
* **Mitigation:** report per ticker-day, and never annualize a Sharpe.

### Old data
* **Direction:** not representative of today's market.
* **Why:** the data is NASDAQ, 2019-01-30, the day after Apple's earnings.
* **Mitigation:** state it plainly.

### The orderbook file is generated by this project's book
* **Direction:** not a bias, a limit on validation.
* **Why:** the parity check is a consistency check, not an independent one.
* **Mitigation:** the book was validated independently against ITCH
  executions (`docs/design.md`).

## Session handling

* **Halts and crosses (types 6/7):** all strategy orders are pulled, and new
  orders are rejected until the next ordinary message. On the AAPL day this
  happens once, at the 09:30 opening cross. Auction mechanics are not
  modelled.
* **Session end:** the session ends at the last message in the file. Acks
  and fills already decided are delivered; orders still in flight are
  dropped and counted.

## Fees and latency values used

* **Fees:** taker $0.0030 per share and maker rebate $0.0020 per share, in
  the shape of NASDAQ's published per-share pricing. These are placeholders,
  so check the venue's current schedule before quoting a number.
* **Latency:** 20 µs feed, 10 µs processing, 50 µs to the exchange, 50 µs
  back, plus uniform jitter up to 20 µs. That is a co-located-style
  assumption; the sweep covers 0 to 5 ms for order entry.
